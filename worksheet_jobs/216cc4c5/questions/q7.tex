\begin{question}
\noindent In a science laboratory, a researcher tracking a satellite signal has set up a measuring grid on a table to model the positions of two receiver stations, $A$ and $B$, used in a space exploration experiment.

\noindent Station $A$ is positioned 9 cm to the left of a reference point $C$, and Station $B$ is positioned 12 cm above $C$, as shown in the diagram. The angle at $C$ is a right angle.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1, transform shape]
    \coordinate (A) at (0,0);
    \coordinate (B) at (0,4);
    \coordinate (C) at (3,0);

    \draw (A) -- (B) -- (C) -- cycle;

    \draw pic[draw, angle radius=2.5mm] {right angle=B--A--C};

    \node at (A) [below left,font=\small] {A};
    \node at (B) [above left,font=\small] {B};
    \node at (C) [below right,font=\small] {C};

    \draw (A) -- (B) node[midway, left] {12 cm};
    \draw (A) -- (C) node[midway, below] {9 cm};
    \draw (B) -- (C) node[midway, above right] {$d$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\noindent The researcher needs to calculate the straight-line distance $d$, in centimetres, between Station $A$ and Station $B$'s signal path, represented by the direct line $BC$, in order to calibrate the timing of the satellite signal.

\noindent Calculate the distance $BC$.
\vspace{4cm}
\begin{flushright}
    \answerunit{cm}{3}
\end{flushright}
\end{question}
